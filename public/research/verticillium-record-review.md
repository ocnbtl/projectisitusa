# Verticillium albo-atrum: record-by-record evidence review

Reviewed November 4, 2026. Stable catalog ID: verticillium-albo-atrum. Registry occurrence ID: USRIIS-L48-Fungi15529.

## Scope and result

The production catalog contains one registry entry under this name and zero attached county occurrences (snapshot dated September 29, 2026). This review checks that entry, its references, and individual cultures or outbreaks traced through those references. These are not 14 county records, and the review does not create new occurrences.

Five published U.S. cultures and two Oregon outbreak reports have explicit modern identifications in the sources below. That helps interpret the literature; it does not resolve the identity of the broader registry entry. The catalog name and all stable IDs, county data, photographs, and original registry provenance remain unchanged. The classification remains needs-review and the profile evidence remains limited.

## What would resolve the remaining uncertainty?

- **Registry identity:** the exact source record or preserved specimen/culture supporting USRIIS-L48-Fungi15529, with a modern identification that can be traced to it.
- **DSM 12231 conflict:** clarification from the authors and culture collection linking the sequenced material, collection identifier, origin, and current identification. No contact has been sent. Existing public metadata cannot settle this disagreement.
- **ATCC 44943:** a documented reidentification of that particular culture, rather than a guess based on its host.
- **Older distribution aggregates:** original specimens, cultures, sequences, or sufficiently detailed diagnostic records. The 2011 revision explains why old species labels alone cannot reliably separate the modern species.

There is no naming preference that the site owner can choose to resolve these evidence questions. Recommended course: keep the original registry identity and visible limitations until record-specific evidence is available. Contacting a collection or author would be a separate action; this review did not send messages.

## Diagnostic limits

The [2013 identification study](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0065990) describes assays tested on DNA from cultures. That is not a promise that the same tests establish identity directly from every soil or plant sample. Laboratory reports should preserve their exact identification and stated limits.
