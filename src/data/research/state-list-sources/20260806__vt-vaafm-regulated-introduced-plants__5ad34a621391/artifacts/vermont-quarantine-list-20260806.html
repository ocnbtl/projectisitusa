Quarantine List

Species on the Quarantine List are regulated in Vermont. The Rules prohibit cultivation, distribution, transport, and in some cases possession of the quarantined plant pests.  The Agency enforces these standards to prevent introduction or spread but does not actively manage quarantine listed plants that become established. The decision to act on established plants rests with landowners and communities. 

The Quarantine List is organized into three tiers, reflecting the risks each species poses to agriculture, native plants and Vermont’s ecosystems.  These tiers are based on both a species' current distribution status in Vermont and what management can realistically accomplish: 

  • Tier 1 species are not yet present in Vermont. The goal is to keep these plant species from being introduced into Vermont.  
  • Tier 2 species have been introduced and have limited distribution in Vermont. Active management may still make a meaningful difference with prompt action.  
  • Tier 3 species are broadly established across Vermont - this includes  knotweed and garlic mustard.  Local management remains ecologically meaningful, especially to protect areas where displacement of indigenous or native species would otherwise occur.  

Subject matter experts review he Quarantine List annually and recommendations for changes provided to the Secretary. Any updates are publicly posted within 14 days of a decision.  

If you are working with regulated plant material and the acceptable management practices within the Rule cannot be met, a permitting pathway exists. Permits allow work to proceed when the land manager can demonstrate that the regulated species will not lead to further spread. The Agency will evaluate permit requests   on a case-by-case basis.
