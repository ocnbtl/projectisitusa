# Photo follow-up, 28 October 2026

## Scope and results
- Revisited all 98 outstanding photo gaps against GBIF taxonomy and iNaturalist research-grade observations; additionally searched Wikimedia Commons, government identification collections and open research papers.
- 30 gaps filled; 68 remain unresolved. Search results are candidates, not proof of identity. No species, occurrence, research or descriptive data changed.
- Replaced the old spotted-lanternfly photograph: it showed an outreach visit, despite an insect-portrait caption. The new photograph shows an identified adult, credited to Walthery, CC BY-SA 4.0.
- Resolved the other five legacy source links through visual comparison and low normalized pixel error against the source images. The files are resized/recompressed derivatives, not byte-identical originals. Corrected creator/licensing attribution where the old credit was wrong (notably New York State IPM and CSIRO).
- 31 full images and 31 thumbnails added, totaling 668,166 bytes. Existing original files remain intact. New assets use unique versioned filenames.

## Review decisions
- Selected iNaturalist candidates have exact taxon matches, including explicit infraspecific rank normalization and GBIF-supported synonyms. Every selected photograph was visually inspected.
- Additional synonym evidence: USDA GRIN for Datura inoxia/innoxia; USGS for Cichlasoma urophthalma/Mayaheros urophthalmus; Kew POWO for Hieracium vulgatum/Hieracium lachenalii subsp. lachenalii; Zoological Survey of India for Lamprosema/Nacoleia charesalis.
- Rejected GBIF's fuzzy Lamprosema chagosalis match. Also rejected a high-ranked iNaturalist Mayaheros photo dominated by a bird; selected a photograph with clearly visible fish instead.
- Government microscope images come from IDphy, whose copyright page explicitly permits reuse. The Fusarium figure is CC BY and credits the three authors and publication. Symptom images are labeled as symptoms, rather than portraits of microscopic pathogens.
- Did not publish the generic Lotus pedunculatus candidate because the synonym resolves to a narrower subspecies; nor the blurry Convoluta-on-a-penny image. Previously rejected candidates were not silently re-approved.
- Some Commons searches were rate-limited, including after a slower retry; outcomes preserve this distinction from a successful search with no results. Gnomoniopsis open-paper metadata was available, but the figure download could not be retrieved reliably and was not published. Other rare organisms still lack verified, suitable reusable imagery.
