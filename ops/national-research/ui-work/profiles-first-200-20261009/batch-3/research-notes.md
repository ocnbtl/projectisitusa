# Batch 3 research notes

Reviewed 2026-10-09. Display-only editorial; no occurrence determinations. Each profile cites the source supporting its concrete details. Sources were searched and meaningful pages opened; blocked opens are explicitly noted below. No medical or pesticide directions.

- sipha-maydis: SENASA supports dark color, 1.0-1.9 mm wingless size, hairs, and grass hosts. USDA ARS independently supports black diagnostic color. No inferred local presence.
- tinocallis-ulmiparvifoliae: Blackman and Eastop supports winged coloration, wax pattern, size and Ulmus parvifolia host.
- tuberocephalus-sakurae: Hokkaido Research Organization documents summer curled/red leaves, concealed adults and young, approximately 1.5 mm size. Local Japanese observations used for biology only.
- uroleucon-picridis: Blackman and Eastop supports coloration and Picris flower-stem colonies. Explicitly retains caution about non-Picris host misidentifications.
- aphidius-ervi: Kansas State publication distinguishes species-specific host inspection and development. Did not copy its apparent foxglove aphid genus typo.
- aphis-pomi: UC IPM supports seasonal appearance, egg sites, shoot feeding, honeydew and natural enemies.
- mentha-suaveolens: Kew species account supports morphology, creeping habit, damp habitat and garden spread; no blanket local invasive claim.
- nicandra-physalodes: Missouri State weed account supports all morphology and crop context; treatment advice omitted.
- cassia-javanica: NYBG monograph supports paired leaflets, pink/carmine flowers fading pale, and cylindrical pods. Species account kept broad because subspecies differ.
- cassia-javanica-var-indochinensis: NYBG narrative's substantial indexed text supports smaller stipules/sepals and horticultural use; direct narrative opens repeatedly timed out. Successfully opened the related NYBG species monograph and Bishop Museum synonym record. Bishop treats this name under C. javanica subsp. agnes; other treatments vary. Retained queue ID and qualified the taxonomic statement. This is the one profile with a botanical diagnostic comparison supported by indexed primary text rather than a successful direct page open.
- phenacoccus-aceris: ScaleNet supports wax coating, marginal filaments, body color and ovisac; Washington State University supports honeydew/sooty mold context.
- jasminum-sambac: Royal Botanic Gardens Victoria supports evergreen climbing habit, leaves, fragrant white flowers and double cultivars. Kew corroborates accepted species and native range; no local spread inferred.
- schismus-arabicus: UC Weed Research and Information Center PDF directly supports tufted annual habit, fine foliage, dense panicles, seed reproduction/tumbling and qualified fire effects. Cal-IPC supports difficult separation from S. barbatus. Removed the Flora of China microscopic comparison after its page repeatedly timed out.
- brachyrhynchus-membranaceus: USDA ARS research abstract supports flattened form, fungus feeding and damage to cultivated bracket fungi; did not turn an historical record into a present distribution claim.
- neophyllaphis-araucariae: Blackman and Eastop supports yellow/orange color, small oval body and young Araucaria growth; no claimed certainty about origin.
- lindingaspis-rossi: ScaleNet supports dark, flattened female cover and near-central cast skin, leaf use and broad hosts. Catalog discusses cryptic lineages; profile does not imply all populations are identical.
- opuntia-leucotricha: Royal Botanic Garden Sydney PlantNET supports upright trunk, gray-green pads, fine hairs and flexible pale spines.
- linepithema-humile: UC IPM supports uniformly sized brown workers, shallow nests, multiple queens and large connected colonies.
- salvator-merianae: UF IFAS publication supports size, black/white pattern, omnivory, daytime activity, burrows and winter dormancy. Dated legal rules and exact current range omitted.
- lepidium-bonariense: Flora of New Zealand supports annual/biennial habit, hairiness, deeply cut lower leaves, pale flowers and notched fruits; lower leaves may be lost by flowering.
- aonidiella-taxus: Naturalis armored-scale account supports buff/translucent cover, approximately 2 mm size, female color with maturity and conifer hosts; species-level identification remains specialized.
- dynaspidiotus-pseudomeyeri: ScaleNet supports cover/body appearance and Chamaecyparis, Juniperus and Thuja hosts; retained queue genus despite historical Nuculaspis usage.
- gymnaspis-aechmeae: ScaleNet supports flyspeck appearance, retained second-stage skin, bromeliad/greenhouse association and live-born crawlers.
- howardia-biclavis: ScaleNet explicitly disputes whether the scale actively burrows or settles in crevices and becomes covered by tissue. Summary preserves that uncertainty rather than repeating the older active-burrowing explanation.
- lepidosaphes-pinnaeformis: Naturalis supports brown mussel-shaped covers, orchid association, leaf/stem feeding and discoloration; mobility distinguished between crawlers and settled females.
- lepidosaphes-yanagicola: UMass insect guide supports brown oyster-shaped covers, white females, twig use and leaf midrib colonization at higher densities.
- neopinnaspis-harperi: Naturalis supports narrow tan covers, pink/red female body and bark crevices on woody hosts. No invented life-cycle timing.
- parlatoria-camelliae: ScaleNet supports small oval pale covers with dark cast skin, and camellia leaf surfaces near veins; seasonal observations were not generalized nationally.
- rutherfordia-major: ScaleNet supports tissue-obscured cover, variable apparent red/brown/black coloration, marginal cast skin and broad woody hosts including lychee.
- selenaspidus-albus: ScaleNet supports white circular cover, yellow-brown subcentral cast skins and Euphorbia hosts; no generalized economic severity or treatment advice.
- selenaspidus-articulatus: Naturalis supports flattened translucent cover, subcentral cast skins, leaf use and yellowing; citrus is an important host but not the only one.
- unaspis-citri: UF IFAS supports snow-like male covers, contrasting female covers, trunk/limb feeding and bark injury in heavy populations.
- andaspis-punicae: Current ScaleNet places this taxon in Lepidosaphes as L. punicae. Preserved queue ID, explained synonym in summary. USDA ARS supports lychee twig/branch infestation and dieback; did not claim the scale causes the associated corky bark condition because causation is uncertain.
- fiorinia-proboscidaria: ScaleNet supports elongated golden-brown ridged cover, adult retained in enlarged immature skin, leaf-underside colonies and citrus host; no quantified local harm inferred.
- dichrostachys-cinerea: Kew flora treatments support spines, finely divided leaves, two-color fertile/sterile flower-head sections, twisted clustered pods and suckering thickets. No blanket invasion claim.
- paspalum-paniculatum: Kew flora treatments support coarse perennial clumps, broad blades, numerous racemes, and forest-clearing/plantation habitats.
- pseudosasa-japonica: Flora of New Zealand supports glossy broad leaves, peripheral running rhizomes, dense thickets and garden escapes at roadsides, riverbanks and forest margins; no new occurrence assertion.
- xanthosoma-sagittifolium: NParks supports large arrow leaves with basal stalk attachment, thick stem, shaded moist but not flooded habitat, and spadix/spathe. No food preparation advice.
- caladium-bicolor: Kew flora descriptions support tuberous habit, peltate heart/arrow leaves, cultivated color variation and floral spike/bract.
- diplosoma-listerianum: Smithsonian NEMESIS supports thin colonies with shared tunic and common exit openings, filter feeding and artificial fouling surfaces. Native origin is uncertain and omitted.
- polyandrocarpa-zorritensis: Smithsonian NEMESIS supports club-shaped individuals, connecting stolons, individual tunics, sand coating and budding. Did not equate its Peruvian type locality with a known native range.
- siphoninus-phillyreae: UF IFAS supports white-waxed adults, leaf-underside nymphs, characteristic wax tufts/glassy projections, broad tree hosts and damage under heavy infestation; no current regional eradication claim.
- potentilla-inclinata: German Federal Agency for Nature Conservation FloraWeb directly supports hairy stems, 5-7 toothed leaflets, gray undersides, yellow flowers and dry sandy/disturbed habitat. WFO indexed descriptions corroborate perennial habit.
- sphaerodactylus-elegans: Heagy and Frank (2008), Iguana, directly supports dramatic juvenile/adult color change, moist cover, daytime/twilight activity and climbing. Used natural-history observations, not captive-care prescriptions. An older UF gecko-key URL now redirects to an unrelated snake page and was not cited.
- eriocaulon-cinereum: Kew Flora of Tropical East Africa treatment supports tufted annual, narrow leaves, slender stems, gray/straw heads, drying pools/seepages and rice association. No precise native boundary asserted because treatments differ.
- polypogon-fugax: Kew GrassBase and Flora of Somalia support annual habit, rooting nodes, rough blades, narrow dense panicle and fine short awns.
- aedes-japonicus: UF IFAS account is specifically A. japonicus japonicus, the widespread introduced subspecies. Used recognizable coloration, container development and cool tolerance; no disease transmission or medical advice.
- dryocosmus-kuriphilus: UF IFAS supports green/pink chestnut galls, woody persistence, asexual female reproduction and effects on shoots/flowers/yield. No unsupported numerical losses or present county claims.
- diaphorina-citri: UC IPM supports tilted adults, mottled coloring, young shoot preference, nymphal wax tubes and transmission of the HLB-associated bacterium. No pesticide or regulatory directions.
- blattella-asahinai: UF IFAS supports long wings, strong flight, outdoor mulch/litter habitat, attraction to light and rare indoor infestation. Avoided unsupported identification from color alone.
