# First 200 species profiles

Source-checked descriptions for owner review. These describe species biology; they do not establish local presence.

## 1. Absinthium

*Artemisia absinthium* | Batch 1

Wormwood's silvery, finely divided leaves give this perennial a soft appearance, while its strong scent is another useful clue. Lower leaves are larger and more divided than those near the tips of its branching stems. Originally from Eurasia, it is now naturalized across northern North America, including open fields and roadsides in South Dakota.

- [South Dakota State University](https://openprairie.sdstate.edu/nativeplant/203/)

## 2. Achicoria azul

*Launaea intybacea* | Batch 1

Despite the name achicoria azul, this plant has pale yellow flowers. Slender, branching stems carry many small flower heads above toothed or lobed leaves, and ripe fruits have a tuft of white bristles. It grows in open, often disturbed places, including dry riverbeds, rocky ground and cultivated land. The flower color is a helpful starting point when comparing it with blue-flowered chicories.

- [Kew · Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:229022-1/general-information)

## 3. Acoellous turbellarian flatworm

*Convoluta convoluta* | Batch 1

A flatworm only about 2-3 millimeters long, found on algae and other shallow-water surfaces. Researchers studied its feeding on newly settled mussels in the Gulf of Maine; those findings describe that study, not every coastline.

- [Byrnes & Witman · Gulf of Maine study](https://byrneslab.net/pdfs/Byrnes_and_Witman_2003_JEMBE.pdf)

## 4. Active gray pine needle aphid

*Eulachnus rileyi* | Batch 1

This slender aphid feeds on pine needles. A dusting of bluish-gray wax can conceal an olive or orange-brown body, so color alone is not enough to distinguish it from other pine aphids.

- [Iowa State University · BugGuide](https://www.bugguide.net/node/view/484519)

## 5. Acuminate scale

*Kilifia acuminata* | Batch 1

A soft scale insect associated with mango and other host plants. It belongs to the group of sap-feeding insects that can be overlooked on foliage; a close examination is needed to separate it from other mango scales.

- [University of Florida · Mango pests](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/IG073)

## 6. Adelgid

*Pineus boerneri* | Batch 1

Small reddish-brown insects shelter under woolly, gray-white wax on pine twigs. The wax is often easier to notice than the adelgids themselves; related pine adelgids can look very similar.

- [Blackman & Eastop · Aphids on the World’s Plants](https://aphidsonworldsplants.info.aphidnet.org/d_aphids_P/)

## 7. Aegean wallflower

*Erysimum cheiri* | Batch 1

A familiar garden wallflower with clusters of yellow or orange blooms, though cultivated varieties come in other colors. It can grow in rocky crevices and walls where the soil drains freely.

- [NC State Extension · Wallflower](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/erysimum-x-cheiri/)

## 8. African amaranth

*Amaranthus muricatus* | Batch 1

A low-growing amaranth with narrow leaves, dense green flower clusters and tiny, wrinkled fruits. Its common name is misleading: this plant originated in South America, not Africa.

- [Flora of Israel · Botanical description](https://flora.org.il/en/plants/amamur/)

## 9. African asparagus fern

*Asparagus asparagoides* | Batch 1

Bridal creeper scrambles over nearby plants with wiry stems, glossy oval leaf-like shoots and berries that ripen red. Its delicate-looking growth hides a substantial network of roots and tubers underground. In invaded Australian habitats, both the climbing stems and dense root mats crowd out other vegetation. Birds can carry seeds in the fruit, while discarded roots or contaminated soil can start new patches.

- [NSW Government · Bridal creeper](https://weeds.dpi.nsw.gov.au/Weed/BridalCreeper)

## 10. African bermudagrass

*Cynodon nlemfuensis* | Batch 1

This star grass spreads along the ground on long runners, forming a dense mat. Unlike common bermudagrass, it lacks underground rhizomes. Its finger-like flower spikes are a useful clue, but closely related grasses need careful comparison.

- [Tropical Forages · Cynodon](https://tropicalforages.info/text/entities/cynodon_spp.htm)

## 11. African blackchin tilapia

*Sarotherodon melanotheron* | Batch 1

A tilapia adapted to estuaries and brackish lagoons. In Florida, records also come from canals and drainage ditches. Males shelter developing eggs in their mouths, a distinctive part of this fish’s breeding behavior.

- [USGS · Blackchin tilapia](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/FactSheet.aspx?SpeciesID=477)

## 12. African bowstring hemp

*Sansevieria cylindrica* | Batch 1

Often sold as a cylindrical snake plant, this succulent grows stiff, rounded leaves from a basal rosette. You may also find it named Dracaena angolensis; that name refers to the same plant in the linked botanical reference.

- [NC State Extension · African spear](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/dracaena-angolensis/)

## 13. African boxthorn

*Lycium ferocissimum* | Batch 1

A woody shrub armed with long thorns, small fleshy leaves and orange-red berries. It can form dense thickets. Photograph its leaves, flowers and branching pattern from a safe distance; the thorns make close handling difficult.

- [NSW Government · African boxthorn](https://weeds.dpi.nsw.gov.au/Weed/AfricanBoxthorn)

## 14. African bristlegrass

*Setaria sphacelata* | Batch 1

A perennial grass that grows in dense clumps, sometimes reaching two meters. Its long, bristly flower spikes and broad grass blades help narrow an identification, but several other Setaria grasses have a similar outline.

- [Royal Botanic Gardens Sydney · PlantNET](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=sp&name=Setaria~sphacelata&page=nswfl)

## 15. African clawed frog

*Xenopus laevis* | Batch 1

An aquatic frog with a flattened body, eyes on top of its head and fully webbed hind feet. Three toes on each hind foot carry dark claws. Its front fingers are unwebbed, unlike its broad swimming feet.

- [USGS · African clawed frog](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/FactSheet.aspx?SpeciesID=67)

## 16. African cornflag

*Chasmanthe floribunda* | Batch 1

Sword-shaped leaves rise beneath tall, branching spikes of orange flowers. This South African plant grows from an underground corm and is easily confused with Crocosmia, so the shape and arrangement of its flowers matter.

- [SANBI · PlantZAfrica](https://pza.sanbi.org/chasmanthe-floribunda)

## 17. African feathergrass

*Cenchrus macrourus* | Batch 1

Tall clumps carry long, narrow flower spikes surrounded by fine bristles. This grass spreads through underground stems as well as seeds; moving soil or discarded garden material can move those stems to new places.

- [NSW Government · African feather grass](https://weeds.dpi.nsw.gov.au/Weed/AfricanFeatherGrass)

## 18. African fig fly

*Zaprionus indianus* | Batch 1

A small brown fruit fly with crisp white stripes edged in black along its head and back. Those stripes help distinguish it from many familiar vinegar flies; close-up photographs are more useful than a distant view.

- [Virginia Tech · African fig fly](https://virginiafruit.ento.vt.edu/AFF.html)

## 19. African Jewelfish

*Hemichromis letourneuxi* | Batch 1

A small African cichlid found in fresh and brackish water. Its identification has a complicated history: older Florida records often used the name Hemichromis bimaculatus, making the source and scientific name especially important.

- [USGS · African jewelfish](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/factsheet.aspx?SpeciesID=457)

## 20. African jointgrass

*Hemarthria altissima* | Batch 1

Also known as limpograss, this perennial is grown as forage on wet, poorly drained ground in Florida. It keeps growing into cooler parts of the season when many other warm-season pasture grasses slow down.

- [University of Florida · Limpograss](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/AG330)

## 21. African mustard

*Strigosella africana* | Batch 1

A small annual mustard with pink to violet flowers and long, slender seed pods. Its hairy stems often branch close to the ground. Look for the combination of flowers and pods when comparing it with other roadside mustards.

- [Intermountain Herbaria · Botanical description](https://intermountainbiota.org/portal/taxa/index.php?taxon=1205)

## 22. African oil palm

*Elaeis guineensis* | Batch 1

A large palm with arching fronds and tightly packed bunches of oily fruits. Young trunks retain the bases of old leaves. Native to tropical Africa, it is the species cultivated widely for palm oil.

- [Kew · Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:666802-1/general-information)

## 23. African olive

*Olea europaea ssp. cuspidata* | Batch 1

An evergreen olive with a dense crown and small fruits that turn purple-black. Its leaves are green to yellow-brown underneath, unlike the silvery underside of common cultivated olives. Birds can disperse its seeds.

- [NSW Government · African olive](https://weeds.dpi.nsw.gov.au/Weed/AfricanOlive)

## 24. African pepperwort

*Lepidium africanum* | Batch 1

A pepperwort with tiny flowers and rows of small, flattened seed pods. The lower leaves are often toothed or divided, while leaves higher on the stem become narrower. Mature pods help distinguish it from other pepperweeds.

- [Royal Botanic Gardens Sydney · PlantNET](https://plantnet.rbgsyd.nsw.gov.au/cgi-bin/NSWfl.pl?lvl=sp&name=Lepidium~africanum&page=nswfl)

## 25. African rock python

*Python sebae* | Batch 1

A large, nonvenomous constrictor introduced to southern Florida. African rock pythons can resemble Burmese pythons, so a photograph and expert identification are more useful than a guess from size alone. Keep your distance from any large unfamiliar snake.

- [Florida Museum · African rock python](https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/florida-snake-id/snake/african-rock-python/)

## 26. African senna

*Senna didymobotrya* | Batch 1

Often sold as popcorn cassia, this African shrub gets its nickname from the scent of its rubbed foliage. It grows quickly and can become a small tree in warm climates, while gardeners in colder regions often grow it as a seasonal ornamental.

- [University of Wisconsin Extension](https://hort.extension.wisc.edu/articles/popcorn-cassia-senna-cassia-didymobotrya/)

## 27. African tamarisk

*Tamarix africana* | Batch 1

This tamarisk carries exceptionally small leaves: individual blades are only about 1.5-2.5 millimeters long. Identifying a tamarisk to species takes more than its feathery appearance; retain close photographs of leaves and flowers for comparison with a botanical key.

- [Flora of North America](https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=250101263)

## 28. African tuliptree

*Spathodea campanulata* | Batch 1

Large orange-red flowers with crinkled yellow edges make African tulip trees easy to notice. Long capsules split to release light, winged seeds, and damaged trees can produce root suckers. In tropical and subtropical regions it can spread beyond planted landscapes into wetter disturbed habitats.

- [Environmental Weeds of Australia · Botanical key](https://keyserver.lucidcentral.org/weeds/data/03080008-0301-4c05-8c0e-0c0f040b0803/media/Html/spathodea_campanulata.htm)

## 29. African warthog

*Phacochoerus africanus* | Batch 1

A broad head, curved tusks and a bristly mane give the common warthog its familiar outline. This African grassland animal often lowers itself onto padded wrist joints to crop short grass, then uses its snout and tusks to dig for bulbs and roots. Mud wallows help it cool down, and burrows provide shelter at night. Females and young commonly live in family groups.

- [University of Michigan · Animal Diversity Web](https://animaldiversity.org/accounts/Phacochoerus_africanus/)

## 30. Air yam

*Dioscorea bulbifera* | Batch 1

Air potato is a climbing vine with broad, heart-shaped leaves and potato-like bulbils held above ground along its stems. Those bulbils are a useful recognition clue. In Florida, vines can climb into tree canopies, so look up as well as along fences and woodland edges.

- [University of Florida · Air potato](https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/dioscorea-bulbifera/)

## 31. Alder rust

*Melampsoridium hiratsukanum* | Batch 1

Alder rust can leave orange-yellow pustules beneath alder leaves, with yellow or orange spots above. Leaves may curl, brown or fall early. Several alder rust fungi look alike, so these symptoms justify closer examination rather than a species-level diagnosis from a photograph.

- [California Department of Food and Agriculture](https://blogs.cdfa.ca.gov/Section3162/?p=1226)

## 32. Aleppo pine

*Pinus halepensis* | Batch 1

Aleppo pine starts with a relatively narrow crown that becomes rounded or umbrella-shaped as it ages. Its thin, light-green needles usually grow in pairs, and cones can stay on the branches for several years. Native to the Mediterranean and widely planted in California and the Southwest, it tolerates poor soil, heat and coastal conditions. A twisted trunk and irregular branching add to its distinctive silhouette.

- [Oregon State University · Landscape Plants](https://landscapeplants.oregonstate.edu/plants/pinus-halepensis)

## 33. Alexander palm

*Ptychosperma elegans* | Batch 1

Also called solitaire palm, this Australian palm has a single slim, pale trunk marked with rings. Look for jagged, squared-off leaflet tips, a smooth green crownshaft and bright red fruit. Despite the common name Alexander palm, the scientific name matters when comparing it with other ornamental palms.

- [University of Florida · Solitaire palm](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FR313)

## 34. Alexandrian laurel

*Calophyllum inophyllum* | Batch 1

Look closely at a beauty leaf tree and you will see many fine, parallel veins running across its glossy oval leaves. Fragrant white flowers give way to round fruits, each containing a seed surrounded by a buoyant layer. That packaging helps the fruits travel on water. Also called tamanu, this tropical tree grows along sandy and rocky shores and is planted for shade.

- [Singapore National Parks · Flora & Fauna Web](https://www.nparks.gov.sg/florafaunaweb/flora/2/7/2774)
- [National Tropical Botanical Garden](https://www.ntbg.org/database/plants/detail/calophyllum-inophyllum)

## 35. Alfalfa

*Medicago sativa* | Batch 1

Alfalfa is the familiar perennial legume grown for hay and livestock feed. Its leaves have three leaflets, and clusters of small, pea-shaped flowers are usually bluish purple, though other colors occur. The developing pods curl into tight spirals, a useful detail when flowers are gone. Plants can also occur along roadsides and field edges, so note whether a patch is cultivated or growing on its own.

- [University of Minnesota Extension · Forage legumes](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/forages/forage-legumes)
- [University of Minnesota Extension · Alfalfa flowers](https://blog-crop-news.extension.umn.edu/2024/10/the-beauty-of-alfalfa.html)
- [University of British Columbia - E-Flora BC](https://linnet.geog.ubc.ca/Atlas/Atlas.aspx?noTransfer=1&sciname=Medicago+sativa)

## 36. Alfalfa

*Medicago sativa ssp. sativa* | Batch 1

This is the purple- or blue-flowered subspecies of alfalfa, the forage plant also called lucerne. Like other alfalfa, it has three-part leaves and compact clusters of pea-shaped flowers; its pods form little coils as they develop. Those flower and fruit details help separate it from yellow-flowered relatives. This entry preserves the subspecies distinction, rather than treating every alfalfa observation as equally precise.

- [University of British Columbia - E-Flora BC](https://linnet.geog.ubc.ca/Atlas/Atlas.aspx?noTransfer=1&sciname=Medicago+sativa)

## 37. Algae

*Gracilaria vermiculophylla* | Batch 1

This branching red seaweed can form bushy tangles of narrow, cylindrical strands. Its color ranges from reddish brown to greenish or nearly black, making color an unreliable shortcut. Closely related seaweeds look similar; reliable identification can require microscopy or genetic analysis.

- [Smithsonian Environmental Research Center · NEMESIS](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/-328)

## 38. Algae

*Grateloupia doryphora* | Batch 1

Use this name carefully when reading older seaweed records. Large red algae introduced to Europe and New England were once called Grateloupia doryphora, but genetic work identified them as G. turuturu. A historical label should therefore be checked before transferring its distribution or impacts to this species.

- [Smithsonian Environmental Research Center · Identification history](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/12572)

## 39. Algerian sea lavender

*Limonium ramosissimum* | Batch 1

Algerian sea lavender is a management concern in San Francisco Bay tidal marshes. It is easily confused with European sea lavender, so photographs alone may not settle an identification. The distinction matters when recording a patch, comparing older surveys or planning work around sensitive marsh habitat.

- [California Invasive Plant Council · Sea lavender research](https://www.cal-ipc.org/project/sea-lavender-sf-bay/)
- [Cal-IPC · Similar-species caution](https://www.cal-ipc.org/plants/paf/limonium-duriusculum-plant-assessment-form/)

## 40. Alligatorweed

*Alternanthera philoxeroides* | Batch 1

Alligatorweed spreads across water and wet banks on sprawling stems. Look for opposite leaves and small, white, papery flower heads held on stalks; aquatic stems are often hollow. Stem fragments can root, which is why broken pieces deserve attention when cleaning equipment or moving plant material.

- [University of Florida · Alligatorweed](https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/alternanthera-philoxeroides/)
- [University of Florida IFAS · Weed management](https://ipm.ifas.ufl.edu/pdfs/Chapter6-Weed_Mgmt.pdf)

## 41. Allium leafminer

*Phytomyza gymnostoma* | Batch 1

Rows of tiny white punctures on onion, leek or garlic leaves are an early clue to allium leafminer activity. Adult females make the marks, while larvae feed inside the plant. Photograph both the damaged leaf and the whole crop before seeking confirmation, since not every damaged onion has this pest.

- [Penn State Extension · Allium leafminer](https://extension.psu.edu/fall-flight-of-allium-leafminer-observed-in-southeast-pa)

## 42. Allspice

*Pimenta dioica* | Batch 1

The allspice in a kitchen spice jar comes from the dried, unripe fruits of this tree, not a blend of several spices. The living plant is a tropical evergreen with aromatic leaves, smooth gray bark and clusters of small white flowers. Its berries turn from green to purple as they ripen. Native to southern Mexico, Central America and the Caribbean, it is especially associated with cultivation in Jamaica.

- [Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew - Species profile](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:196799-2/general-information)

## 43. Almond moth

*Cadra cautella* | Batch 1

Almond moth is a stored-food pest whose larvae can infest bagged grain and the surface of bulk grain stores. The name is narrower than its food supply: an infestation is not limited to almonds. When moths appear around stored food, identifying the infested product is as important as identifying the adult.

- [Canadian Grain Commission · Almond moth](https://www.canada.ca/en/grain-commission/services/grain-quality/manage-stored-grain/identify-insect/secondary-insect-pests/almond-moth.html)

## 44. Aloe aphid

*Aloephagus myersi* | Batch 1

Tiny aloe aphids can hide beneath leaf bases, where their fluffy wax may be more visible than their bodies. Wingless adults are roughly 2 millimeters long and may be attended by ants. Inspect sheltered parts of an aloe rather than only its exposed leaf surfaces.

- [Blackman & Eastop · Aphids on the World's Plants](https://aphidsonworldsplants.info.aphidnet.org/d_aphids_A/)

## 45. Alsike clover

*Trifolium hybridum* | Batch 1

Alsike clover has white-to-pink flower heads and smooth leaflets without the pale watermark often seen on other clovers. It favors moist ground and can reseed into pastures and roadsides. Despite its scientific name, it is a distinct species, not a hybrid of red and white clover.

- [University of Minnesota Extension · Forage legumes](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/crop-production/forages/forage-legumes)

## 46. Alyssum

*Alyssum strigosum* | Batch 1

An annual alyssum native from southern Europe toward Afghanistan. Its name needs particular care in North American records: Flora of North America notes that many reports were based on misidentified A. simplex. Check the specimen or original identification before treating an old record as confirmed.

- [Flora of North America · Alyssum identification notes](https://efloras.org/florataxon.aspx?flora_id=1&taxon_id=241000086)
- [Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:277808-1)

## 47. Amaranth aphid

*Aphis amaranthi* | Batch 1

Amaranth aphids are small olive-green or reddish-brown insects associated with amaranths. Colonies can occur around roots and stem bases as well as leaves and flower clusters, and ants often attend them. A close look near the base of a plant may reveal insects missed by a quick leaf check.

- [Blackman & Eastop · Aphids on the World's Plants](https://aphidsonworldsplants.info.aphidnet.org/d_aphids_A/)

## 48. Amazon sailfin catfish

*Pterygoplichthys pardalis* | Batch 1

An armored suckermouth catfish often called an Amazon sailfin catfish. Dark markings on its underside help distinguish it from related species, but the patterns can vary and should not be used alone. Several similar aquarium catfishes have been introduced outside their native range, making careful identification important.

- [US Army Corps of Engineers · Suckermouth catfishes](https://ansrp.el.erdc.dren.mil/elpubs/pdf/ansrp-v14-1.pdf)

## 49. Ambay pumpwood

*Cecropia pachystachya* | Batch 1

Ambay pumpwood has large, deeply lobed leaves and a lightly branched crown that give it an umbrella-like outline. Native to tropical South America, it is a pioneer tree that takes advantage of open or disturbed ground. Its relationship with ants is especially interesting: hollow stems offer shelter, and specialized structures provide food. Similar Cecropia trees share much of this appearance, so a leaf silhouette alone will not settle the species.

- [Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew · Plants of the World Online](https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:50908-2)
- [National University of Singapore - Cecropia ecology](https://lkcnhm.nus.edu.sg/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/app/uploads/2017/06/2010nis199-209.pdf)
- [Reis and colleagues - Ant fauna on Cecropia pachystachya](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=5ac39ae896c6435f15b9f39395ecc8a6291b6e5f&repid=rep1&type=pdf)

## 50. Ambrosia beetle

*Anisandrus maiche* | Batch 1

This tiny ambrosia beetle makes its home inside wood, where it cultivates fungi that feed its young. North American records were first published in 2009 from Pennsylvania, Ohio and West Virginia. Research in central New York found that it spends winter as a mature larva inside its galleries. Small entry holes may draw attention to an affected tree, but identifying this beetle requires a closer look at the insect itself.

- [USDA Agricultural Research Service · First North American records](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=235932)
- [USDA ARS - Phenology and overwintering study](https://www.ars.usda.gov/research/publications/publication/?seqNo115=417167)

## 51. Ambrosia beetle

*Euwallacea similis* | Batch 2

This slender, light-brown ambrosia beetle is a wood inhabitant with a broad range of host trees. Its narrow shape and the small bumps on the sloping rear of its wing covers help specialists separate it from similar beetles. Records often use the older name Xyleborus similis, so tracking those names matters when comparing reports of this introduced species.

- [Netherlands NPPO: Euwallacea similis risk scan](https://english.nvwa.nl/binaries/nvwa-en/documenten/plant/plant-health/pest-risk-analysis/documents/quick-scan-euwallacea-similis/Quick%2Bscan-Euwallacea-similis-January-2022.pdf)
- [University of Florida and US Forest Service: North American Xyleborini](https://ambrosiasymbiosis.org/northamericanxyleborini/list.html)

## 52. American evergreen

*Syngonium podophyllum* | Batch 2

Familiar as an indoor arrowhead plant, American evergreen becomes a vigorous climbing vine in warm, shaded landscapes. Young leaves have the recognizable arrow shape, while established plants can spread across the ground and climb trees. In Florida natural areas, dense growth can suppress native vegetation and add weight to branches. Discarded garden material is one route by which this ornamental escapes cultivation.

- [UF/IFAS: Arrowhead vine](https://plant-directory.ifas.ufl.edu/plant-directory/syngonium-podophyllum/)

## 53. Amphipod

*Caprella scaura* | Batch 2

Caprella scaura is a small marine amphipod that grips seaweeds, branching animals, and submerged structures. Females brood their young, which initially cling to their mother. Dense introduced populations can crowd other small crustaceans. Its original home remains uncertain, and several similar forms have been grouped under this name, complicating the story of its worldwide spread.

- [Smithsonian NEMESIS: Caprella scaura](https://invasions.si.edu/nemesis/species_summary/95430)

## 54. Amur corktree

*Phellodendron amurense* | Batch 2

Amur corktree takes its name from its soft, deeply furrowed bark. This spreading deciduous tree bears opposite leaves divided into several leaflets, with a strong resinous scent when crushed. Female trees produce clusters of small fruits that turn black. Introduced as a landscape tree from eastern Asia, it can seed beyond cultivation, and the University of Connecticut documents an invasive tendency in that state.

- [University of Connecticut Plant Database: Amur corktree](https://plantdatabase.uconn.edu/detail.php?pid=305)

## 55. Amur goby

*Rhinogobius brunneus* | Batch 2

The Amur goby is a small bottom-dwelling fish with joined pelvic fins that form a suction-cup-like disc. Breeding males can show striking red or blue colors and pale fin edges. USGS records fish under this name from the Columbia River region, but cautions that Rhinogobius brunneus has been used for a complex of similar species. That uncertainty matters when interpreting its life history and introduced range.

- [USGS Nonindigenous Aquatic Species: Amur goby](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/FactSheet.aspx?SpeciesID=2612)

## 56. Amur Honeysuckle

*Lonicera maackii* | Batch 2

Amur honeysuckle is a large deciduous shrub with opposite, pointed leaves and paired flowers that fade from white to yellow. Birds carry the seeds in its red fruits. Its early spring leaves and long autumn growing season help it form dense shade beneath woodland edges and trees. Across invaded parts of eastern North America, that thick understory can crowd out native plants.

- [NC State Extension: Amur honeysuckle](https://plants.ces.ncsu.edu/plants/lonicera-maackii/)

## 57. Anchor worm

*Lernaea cyprinacea* | Batch 2

Anchor worm is a tiny crustacean related to other copepods. After mating, the female embeds an anchor-shaped front end in a freshwater fish, leaving her elongated body and egg sacs outside. Carp and goldfish are frequent hosts, though many other fish can be affected. Attachment wounds and damage to gills make heavy infestations a concern, especially in warm, slow-moving water.

- [UF/IFAS: Lernaea infestations in fish](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA185/pdf)

## 58. Anchored water hyacinth

*Eichhornia azurea* | Batch 2

Anchored water hyacinth is an aquatic perennial whose roots usually remain fixed in mud while its stems and leaves spread across the water. Showy blue-purple flowers rise above the leaves, and the leaf stalks lack the inflated floats familiar from common water hyacinth. It grows in warm, slow-moving rivers, lakes, and marshes. Its dense mats change aquatic habitat, although research on its impacts remains limited.

- [USGS Nonindigenous Aquatic Species: Anchored waterhyacinth](https://nas.er.usgs.gov/queries/FactSheet.aspx?speciesID=2758)

## 59. Angel's tears

*Brugmansia suaveolens* | Batch 2

Angel's tears is a woody South American plant known for its large, hanging trumpet flowers. Their evening fragrance attracts moths, linking the plant's daily rhythm to its nighttime pollinators.
